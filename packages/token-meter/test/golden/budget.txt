The quota is the budget, not an experiment's comparison metric: it arrives in whole percent (about 460–560 requests per 1% step in 2026-11) and is shared by every session, subagent and other client of the account, so no session or arm is charged quota points. A window is one provider's `q5h` or `q7d` reset period; its peak is the highest reading, its lockout the time from the cap (a reading of 100, or a rate-limit error after a reading of 99) to the reset, its time to cap the time from the window's start to the cap. Complete = the window is over and the log reaches back to its start. Retry wait is the sum of the `retryWaitMs` of the window's lines (pi-retry's totals on v1 lines, pi's native retries on v2 lines), whatever failed.

### Quota calibration

Per complete window, peak quota ≈ Σ β_c × API-eq $ of component c (a non-negative least-squares fit; no intercept: a window starts at 0). β = quota points per API-eq $ of the component: 1 would mean the quota weighs a token as its list price does. A flag says the quota now weighs tokens differently from the API prices — the signal to revisit the objective, never an automatic change.

### Local cost

Cost = machine hours × the provider's hourly rate in its currency. Machine hours = the union of the requests' serving intervals [end − `durationMs`, end] (failed requests included: they occupy the machine); request hours = Σ `durationMs`, which an experiment charges its units (concurrent requests add up). The queue share = 1 − machine hours ÷ request hours: with one server slot, above 0 local requests overlapped and queued (their `durationMs` include the wait), so an experiment's per-unit cost is above its share of machine time. A request without `durationMs` adds no time: untimed requests make the cost an undercount, and a provider none of whose requests is timed has no cost. A provider without a rate has no cost either: its wall time (the summed request time) is shown instead.
