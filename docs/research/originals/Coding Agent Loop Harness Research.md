# **Deterministic Harness Engineering for Small Autonomous Coding Agents: Loop Detection, Intervention Adherence, and Tiered Escalation**

When deploying a local, open-weight language model of the 7B to 35B parameter class (such as Qwen 2.5/3 Coder) as the primary execution engine for an autonomous coding agent, runtime stability depends heavily on the deterministic harness. Constrained model capacity, vulnerability to context pollution, and the absence of intrinsic self-correction frequently manifest as repetitive tool invocations, unmutated workspace edits, and degenerative error cycling. To prevent computational thrashing within a single local inference slot, the supervising harness must enforce deterministic detection, execute non-advisory intervention boundaries, and govern escalation to frontier models.

## **Behavioral Adherence Under Harness Interventions**

A fundamental design choice in autonomous agent harnesses is whether to deliver advisory feedback through textual notes appended to tool observations and system prompts, or to enforce hard execution blocks, context truncations, and execution halts. Empirical research into language model reasoning dynamics demonstrates that small open models rarely correct course through advisory feedback alone.

### **The Breakdown of Intrinsic Self-Correction**

The premise of advisory feedback is that an autonomous model can perceive an error notice, reflect upon its prior actions, and formulate a viable alternative plan. However, empirical findings demonstrate that language models possess severe structural limitations when attempting intrinsic self-correction without external ground-truth verifiers.  
In a landmark evaluation, Huang et al. (ICLR 2024\) systematically tested the self-correction capabilities of language models across standard reasoning benchmarks in their paper *Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet* (https\://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798) \[Peer-Reviewed\]1. The authors established that when models are prompted to review and self-correct their own reasoning without oracle correctness labels, their accuracy does not improve and frequently deteriorates (Section 3\) \[Peer-Reviewed\]3. Specifically, on the GSM8K mathematical reasoning benchmark, GPT-3.5 retained its initial answer 74.7% of the time; among instances where the answer changed, the model converted a correct answer to an incorrect one in 8.8% of cases, while repairing an incorrect answer to a correct one in only 7.6% of cases (Section 3.1) \[Peer-Reviewed\]4. Huang et al. demonstrated that post-hoc self-correction prompts act as arbitrary perturbations that bias the model away from its initial generation distribution, concluding that intrinsic self-correction without external verification is ineffective for complex reasoning \[Peer-Reviewed\]4.  
Complementary research by Zhang et al. (ACL Findings 2024), titled *Small Language Models Need Strong Verifiers to Self-Correct Reasoning* (https\://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17140), investigated the scaling limits of self-refinement in smaller models (7B to 13B parameters) within the SCORE framework \[Peer-Reviewed\]7. Zhang et al. decomposed self-correction into two distinct operational phases: verification (critiquing an initial attempt to detect errors) and refinement (generating a correction conditioned on the critique) (Section 2\) \[Peer-Reviewed\]8. Their experiments revealed that the primary bottleneck in small models is not generation or refinement, but verification \[Peer-Reviewed\]8. Small open models lack the discriminative capacity to independently verify whether a prior step succeeded or failed; when prompted to critique their own trajectories without external verification signals, their verifier F1 scores drop precipitously, causing downstream refinement passes to amplify erroneous premises \[Peer-Reviewed\]8.

### **Context Pollution and Advisory Ineffectiveness**

The practical implications of these cognitive limitations within coding agent harnesses are documented across open-source agent codebases. In developer discussions for OpenHands (Issue \#15087, *Proposal: Replace LLM-prompted loop detection with deterministic trajectory hashing for Actions*, https\://github.com/OpenHands/OpenHands/issues/15087), maintainers analyzed why injecting prompts such as *"You are looping, please stop"* into the context window of CodeActAgent consistently failed to break execution loops \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11. The maintainers identified two structural failure modes: first, textual prompting burns context tokens while the model continues to retry the same failed action; second, the strategy collapses under severe context pollution \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11. By the time the agent receives an advisory warning, its attention window is already saturated with previous failed command executions, stack traces, and repetitive thoughts \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11. Because autoregressive decoders heavily weight tokens present in their immediate history, the model suffers from attentional bias, repeatedly regenerating the very tool invocation that dominates its context \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11.  
Paul Gauthier's empirical documentation for Aider (*Troubleshooting Edit Errors*, https\://aider.chat/docs/troubleshooting/edit-errors.html) identifies a parallel attention-degradation threshold \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]12. Gauthier observed that when context window occupancy surpasses approximately 25,000 tokens, most models experience instruction drift, becoming significantly more prone to disobeying system prompts, ignoring formatting constraints, and generating invalid code modifications \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]12. Weaker and quantized open-weight models display even steeper degradation profiles, rendering soft advisory notes in deep context windows practically inert \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]12.

*Analytical Inference*: For a local Qwen 3.x \~30B model operating within a single inference slot, advisory harness feedback represents a failure mode. Because smaller open models lack intrinsic verification capability and succumb rapidly to context pollution, the harness must intervene as a deterministic operating system: it must block repeated actions at the tool boundary, return structured synthetic errors, and truncate conversation history to the last known productive state whenever a tripwire is triggered \[Inference\].

## **Deterministic Loop and Stall Detectors: Precision, Recall, and Threshold Dynamics**

Building a reliable execution harness requires formalizing tripwires into quantitative detectors capable of intercepting infinite agentic loops without suppressing valid, multi-step engineering procedures.

### **Empirical Precision and Recall in Static and Runtime Monitors**

Measurement of loop detection performance in autonomous agent trajectories spans static structural analysis of execution graphs and real-time sliding-window telemetry.  
In a comprehensive empirical investigation of autonomous agent failures, Hou et al. (July 2026\) published *When Agents Do Not Stop: Uncovering Infinite Agentic Loops in LLM Agents* (arXiv:2607.01641, https\://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01641) \[Preprint\]20. The authors formalized Infinite Agentic Loops (IALs) as structural execution failures where an agentic feedback path repeatedly triggers model, tool, agent, or workflow execution without an effective stopping bound (Section III.A) \[Preprint\]20. Hou et al. engineered IAL-Scan, a static analyzer that constructs an Agentic Loop Dependence Graph (ALDG) across downstream repositories to identify unbounded feedback paths, tool retries, and cyclical dispatch loops (Section III.D) \[Preprint\]20. Evaluating IAL-Scan across 6,549 real-world LLM agent repositories, the system flagged 74 candidate unbounded execution paths; manual ground-truth verification confirmed 68 genuine IAL vulnerabilities across 47 projects, establishing an empirical precision of **91.9%** (Section I) \[Preprint\]20. The authors noted that loop defects occur when developers rely on model outputs to terminate cycles without wrapping the feedback path in strict deterministic counters \[Preprint\]20.  
At runtime, evaluating trajectory degradation requires continuous monitoring over active turns. Sheriff and Aldrich (Carnegie Mellon University, October 2026\) introduced the Long-Horizon Trajectory Integrity Score in *LTIS: A Real-Time Trajectory Integrity Metric for Agentic Loop Engineering* (https\://doi.org/10.1184/R1/34704354) \[Preprint\]24. The LTIS framework calculates a Turn Integrity Score across five canonical agent moves (discover, do, verify, persist, reschedule) via a weighted geometric mean, rolling turn scores up across an exponential decay window of ![][image1] turns with a decay constant ![][image2] (Section IV.B) \[Preprint\]25. Evaluating 3,000 clean and 3,000 contaminated agent trajectories, the rank-based Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic (AUROC) of the decayed integrity score reached **0.940** at 2% trajectory contamination and **1.000** at 8% or higher contamination (Section VI.A) \[Preprint\]25. The authors reported that the 1st percentile of minimum clean decayed LTIS was **0.927**, demonstrating clear statistical separation from the default failure alarm floor of 0.5 (Section VI.A) \[Preprint\]25. Furthermore, Sheriff and Aldrich observed that bursty trajectory failures reduced 120-turn detection from 99.7% to 66.8%, demonstrating that sliding-window monitors must evaluate cumulative degradation rather than isolated turn anomalies \[Preprint\]24.

### **Empirical False Positive Characteristics in Software Engineering Workflows**

When implementing deterministic detectors against the specific tripwires outlined for small coding agents, three primary false positive classes arise:

> 1. **Reasoning-Preamble Mutation with Identical Actions**: In reasoning models such as Qwen 2.5/3 Coder, the model frequently varies its chain-of-thought rationale before issuing an identical or near-identical tool call (Cursor Forum, *False positive loop detection when using custom model qwen3-coder-plus*, https\://forum.cursor.com/t/too-aggressive-loop-detection/147781) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]29. If a harness computes an execution hash over the raw model generation rather than canonicalized (tool\_name, tool\_arguments), superficial changes in reasoning text will cause the detector to miss genuine action loops \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11. Conversely, if the detector triggers purely on semantic thought similarity, it fires false alarms during complex multi-step debugging where repeated reasoning precedes valid, distinct exploratory edits \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16.  
> 2. **Parallel Tool Batching Inaccuracies**: In modern function-calling protocols, agents frequently emit batches of tool calls in a single completion turn (e.g., viewing four files simultaneously). In the OpenHands SDK implementation (stuck\_detector.py), maintainers discovered that if an action-error detector increments its error streak counter on every tool execution event individually, a single response emitting four invalid calls instantly trips a threshold of 3, falsely terminating the run before the agent has ever observed the returned error payload (Issue \#4331, PR \#4380) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16. The detector was corrected to group actions sharing an identical llm\_response\_id into a single logical attempt \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16.  
> 3. **Iterative Terminal Polling and Exploratory Navigation**: Software engineering workflows routinely require iterative, identical-looking queries (e.g., executing a command that polls a background build process, or navigating a dense file hierarchy via successive grep invocations) (OpenHands Issue \#10350) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]32. In OpenHands, setting the context condensation loop detector threshold to 3 caused immediate false positive halts during complex tasks; maintainers were forced to loosen the threshold to 10 repetitions to avoid intercepting valid agent summarization phases (Issue \#7183, PR \#7237) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]27.

*Empirical Gap*: While static analyzers report precise benchmark metrics (91.9% precision in IAL-Scan \[Preprint\]20), no evidence was found in peer-reviewed or preprint literature measuring the standalone precision, recall, and receiver operating characteristics of individual ad-hoc coding heuristics (such as "file edited 3 times in 4 turns" or "check failing twice") on SWE-bench trajectories. In current production systems, these thresholds remain operational practitioner heuristics \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11.

## **Operationalizing Deterministic Progress: Differentiating Stalls from Extended Problem Solving**

A critical engineering objective in autonomous agent harnesses is differentiating an agent that is working slowly through a challenging implementation from an agent that has stalled in an unproductive loop. Relying on simple turn counters or wall-clock timeouts terminates difficult but solvent trajectories prematurely.

### **Deterministic Velocity Markers**

Harnesses capture forward momentum across three primary environmental surfaces: worktree state, verification transitions, and information ingestion.  
In the workspace tree, git diff serves as the ground truth of environmental mutation. In SWE-agent and Mini-SWE-agent, every execution boundary checks the worktree diff; whenever an agent triggers an unavoidable timeout or cost ceiling, the harness extracts whatever partial patch is reflected in git diff and auto-submits it to prevent total run loss \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]15. Edits that result in net-zero modifications—such as an agent modifying a line and subsequently reverting it, or an edit command returning an empty diff because search blocks failed to match (SWE-agent Issue \#1083)—indicate execution stalls \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]33. Tracking byte-level diff deltas and AST modifications separates productive code generation from repetitive file churning \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]15.  
In verification environments, state transitions are measured via exit codes returned by deterministic test runners (e.g., pytest, cargo test) and static linters. A transition from an initial failure state (exit\_code \!= 0\) to a state with fewer failing assertions establishes quantifiable forward progress \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]15. In Sheriff and Aldrich's LTIS framework, the verification score (![][image4]) carries double the weight of standard execution actions in the Turn Integrity Score (Section IV.A) \[Preprint\]25. Trajectories that perform continuous file modifications without corresponding verification spans accumulate "verification debt," driving the decayed trajectory score below the 0.5 alarm threshold \[Preprint\]25.  
In information space, codebase exploration is measured by tracking non-overlapping byte ranges read from disk. In Aider, the harness mitigates blind exploratory loops by constructing an AST repository map (\~1,000 tokens) using Tree-sitter, mapping class signatures and call graphs across the codebase \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]12. This gives the agent structural awareness without requiring repeated cat or grep invocations \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]36. During exploration, progress is defined as the ingestion of novel line ranges; reading the identical file segment three or more times without intervening code changes represents an attention lock \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]37.

A robust deterministic harness must implement a phased velocity monitor: during initial repository navigation, velocity is measured by the non-redundancy of byte-range reads and command outputs; once an edit tool is invoked, velocity tracking shifts to worktree diff non-reversal combined with test suite assertion transitions \[Inference\].

## **Escalation Dynamics: Advisory Overseers versus Autonomous Subtask Handoff**

When deterministic tripwires indicate that a local \~30B model is trapped, the harness can escalate to a frontier model (such as Claude Opus). The system must decide whether to inject the frontier model's analysis as typed advisory notes back into the small model's context, or to hand off execution of the trapped subtask directly.

### **Evidence from Dual-Model Architectures**

Empirical benchmarks evaluating the decoupling of architectural reasoning from mechanical code editing demonstrate the superior efficacy of full subtask delegation.  
In September 2024, Paul Gauthier published benchmark results for Aider's Architect/Editor configuration (*Separating Code Reasoning and Editing*, https\://aider.chat/2024/09/26/architect.html) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]34. The architecture separates software engineering into two distinct operational steps: an Architect model is prompted to reason about the problem, analyze dependencies, and describe the full solution, while an Editor model receives the Architect's solution and translates it into structured file editing instructions (e.g., search/replace blocks or diffs) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]38. On Aider's code editing benchmark, pairing OpenAI's o1-preview as Architect with either DeepSeek or o1-mini as Editor achieved a state-of-the-art score of **85.0% pass rate**, substantially outperforming the solo baseline scores of the individual models (o1-preview solo achieved 74.2%, and GPT-4o solo achieved 72.9%) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]38.  
In January 2025, Aider benchmarked DeepSeek-R1 paired with Claude 3.5 Sonnet (*R1+Sonnet set SOTA on aider's polyglot benchmark*, https\://aider.chat/2025/01/24/r1-sonnet.html) on the Polyglot benchmark (225 Exercism exercises across C++, Go, Java, JavaScript, Python, and Rust) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]34. The R1+Sonnet pairing attained a **64.0% pass rate 1** and a **100.0% well-formed diff rate**, completing the benchmark at a total API cost of **\$13.29**—achieving top-tier performance at a 14-fold cost reduction compared to running OpenAI o1 solo (\$186.00 benchmark cost) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]34. Engineering analyses of multi-model routing demonstrate that delegating planning to an advanced reasoning model while offloading diff application to an instruction-tuned model reduces overall API costs by 30% to 50% relative to running frontier models end-to-end \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]42.

### **Advisory Overseers versus Subtask Delegation**

While frontier reasoning models excel when directing implementation plans, utilizing a frontier model merely as an *advisory critic* to unstick a failing small model in an ongoing session is fundamentally flawed:  
In an advice-only paradigm, the harness prompts Claude Opus to analyze the trajectory and returns typed advisory findings into the local model's prompt stream. This mechanism forces the 30B model to parse external advice while its context window remains polluted with multiple turns of its own prior failures, stack traces, and unfulfilled tool calls \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11. As demonstrated by Huang et al. and Zhang et al., smaller models lack the robust verification capabilities required to align conflicting instructions with flawed prior context \[Peer-Reviewed\]4. The 30B model frequently acknowledges the advice in its reasoning chain but proceeds to repeat the invalid tool syntax or hallucinated file path due to strong autoregressive priming from its own history \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11.  
Conversely, in an autonomous handoff paradigm, the harness intercepts the stall, packages a clean task representation (the initial user prompt, the current git diff, relevant file contents, and the immediate error traceback), and hands execution to the frontier model in an isolated context window \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]36. Architectural analyses comparing Aider and Claude Code emphasize that when complex subtasks fail, isolating execution within an unpolluted context is vital (Codegen, *Aider vs Claude Code*, June 2026, https\://codegen.com/comparisons/aider-vs-claude-code/) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]36. Claude Code executes subtasks by spawning subagents in dedicated, clean context windows, preventing parent context pollution and allowing the specialized model to resolve the blocker directly \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]36. Once the frontier model produces a valid edit, the harness verifies the change against deterministic test gates and returns only the clean result to the main execution loop \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]25.  
*Analytical Inference*: When tripwires detect an unresolvable loop in a local \~30B model, spending tokens on an "advice-only" query to Claude Opus is an inefficient allocation of compute. The harness should execute an operational handoff: extract the current workspace state, truncate the corrupted conversational history, and allow Claude Opus to execute the specific subtask directly within an isolated context window \[Inference\].

## **Session Dynamics: Prompt Habituation, Cooldowns, and Severity Scheduling**

When deterministic warnings or nudges are injected into an agent's multi-turn context repeatedly across an extended session, the system encounters prompt degradation, attention dilution, and warning habituation.

### **Warning Fatigue and Compliance Ceilings**

The phenomenon of warning habituation in language models parallels human computer interaction dynamics under warning fatigue.  
In security research evaluating autonomous mobile GUI agents under AndroidLab (*ICLR 2027 Conference Submission*, https\://openreview.net/pdf?id=F3ja1kB3PD), researchers observed that models subjected to routine, high-frequency permission dialogs and warning prompts experience systematic habituation \[Preprint\]44. Analogous to large-scale empirical findings on browser security warnings (Akhawe & Felt, 2013), language agents repeatedly presented with non-fatal advisory warnings begin to bypass conditional constraints, treating recurring warning tokens as background conversational noise (Section 2\) \[Preprint\]44.  
Furthermore, architectural analyses of agent system prompts (*Agent Patterns: System Prompt Altitude*, https\://agentpatterns.ai/tags/) identify a strict "compliance ceiling" \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]45. As multi-turn conversations expand, the agent's attention becomes diluted across competing directives \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]45. A harness that continuously injects fixed advisory notes (e.g., *"Warning: You have attempted this edit 3 times"*) degrades the signal-to-noise ratio of the context \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]45. As Aider's documentation establishes, exceeding 25k tokens significantly degrades instruction conformity; repeatedly appending repetitive advisory reminders accelerates this token expansion while providing diminishing behavioral impact \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]12.

### **Stateful Cooldowns and Multi-Tiered Severity Policies**

To prevent habituation and avoid intercepting valid iterative problem solving, production harnesses utilize stateful suppression and escalating severity tiers.  
In the OpenHands SDK (stuck\_detector.py), the harness implements a stateful single-strike nudge policy for repeating action-error patterns \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16. The method get\_action\_error\_nudge() is explicitly designed to fire *once and only once* when an error streak reaches the threshold (\_action\_error\_streak \== action\_error\_threshold) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16. To prevent habituation, the detector records the specific event identifier in \_last\_nudged\_error\_event\_id \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16. If the model generates intermediate reasoning-only responses or empty completions without introducing a new action, the error streak freezes; the harness detects that this event was already nudged and suppresses further advisory messages \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16. If the agent subsequently executes the failing action again (\_action\_error\_streak \> action\_error\_threshold), the detector ceases all advisory feedback, marks ConversationExecutionStatus.STUCK, and halts execution deterministically \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11.  
A complementary multi-tiered escalation strategy is implemented in OpenClaw's Tool-Loop Guard (openclawlab.com/en/docs/tools/loop-detection/) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]28:

> 1. **Tier 1 (Soft Dampening with Cooldown)**: Activated when a tool pattern reaches repeatThreshold: 3 \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]28. The harness issues a warning event and applies a temporal cooldown (detectorCooldownMs: 12000, 12 seconds) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]28. This prevents rapid back-to-back firing during legitimate polling or log inspection operations \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]28.  
> 2. **Tier 2 (Pre-Execution Interception)**: If the agent persists with identical tool inputs within its recent history window (historySize: 20), the harness intercepts the call prior to execution, rejects the invocation, and returns a synthetic tool error, avoiding external state mutation \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]28.  
> 3. **Tier 3 (Terminal Circuit Breaker)**: When repetitive actions accumulate to criticalThreshold: 6, the harness trips a terminal circuit breaker, halting execution to eliminate runaway token spend \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]28.

*Analytical Inference*: A deterministic harness supervising a local 30B model must implement a strict, stateful escalation ladder:

* Turn ![][image5] (Tripwire Threshold reached): Dispatch a single, highly structured error notice containing line numbers and rollback diffs; record the event ID to suppress duplicate notices.  
* Turn ![][image6] (Persisted Pattern): Immediately escalate past advisory feedback. Intercept the tool call, raise a hard execution error, and block workspace mutation.  
* Turn ![][image7] (Unresolved Stall): Execute an autonomous handoff to Claude Opus in a fresh context window, or roll back the workspace via git reset and truncate conversation history to the pre-loop node \[Inference\].

## **Architectural Synthesis**

For a deterministic harness governing a local Qwen 3.x-class \~30B model in a single inference slot, the optimal execution architecture balances deterministic interception, stateful escalation, and subtask handoff:

> 1. **Pre-Execution Normalization**: The harness must intercept all outgoing tool calls prior to execution, stripping ephemeral metadata (e.g., call IDs, thoughts) to generate a canonical hash of (tool\_name, arguments) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11. Parallel tool calls within a single completion must be grouped under a single turn index to prevent false streak accumulation \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16.  
> 2. **Deterministic Workspace Validation**: All file modifications must be gated behind automated linters (flake8, tree-sitter) and git diff inspection \[Peer-Reviewed\]13, \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]34. Edits that introduce syntax errors or produce net-zero diffs must be automatically rolled back and returned as execution errors \[Peer-Reviewed\]13, \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]15.  
> 3. **Zero-Advisory Escalation Ladder**: The harness must abandon continuous advisory text injections. When a tripwire fires, allow exactly one structured diagnostic error \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]16. If the identical action recurs, block execution immediately \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]11.  
> 4. **Isolated Frontier Handoff**: When a critical stall occurs, do not query Claude Opus for advisory critique. Instead, bundle the task description, the current worktree diff, and the failing test trace into an isolated context window, delegating the subtask directly to Claude Opus to produce the concrete code patch \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]36.

## **Low-Certainty Empirical Claims and Research Gaps**

In accordance with strict evidence evaluation standards, the following areas possess the lowest empirical certainty:

> 1. **Runtime Tripwire Precision and Recall on Coding Benchmarks**: While static analysis precision has been rigorously quantified (91.9% precision for IAL-Scan across 6,549 repositories \[Preprint\]20) and trajectory scoring has been measured under controlled synthetic contamination (AUROC 0.940–1.000 for LTIS \[Preprint\]25), no evidence was found in peer-reviewed literature measuring the exact precision, recall, and false positive rates of specific runtime heuristics (e.g., "file edited 3 times in 4 turns", "check failing twice") across standard SWE-bench trajectories. These specific thresholds remain uncalibrated practitioner heuristics.  
> 2. **Quantitative Habituation Decay Rates in Open-Weight 30B Models**: While instruction degradation past 25k tokens (Aider) \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]12 and security warning habituation (AndroidLab) \[Preprint\]44 are documented, there is a lack of published empirical data quantifying the exact rate of compliance decay when identical advisory harness notes are repeatedly injected into Qwen 2.5/3 Coder models specifically.  
> 3. **Controlled SWE-Bench Ablation of Advice Injection versus Subtask Handoff**: Although Aider demonstrates that Architect/Editor model pairings achieve superior benchmark accuracy over solo execution (85.0% pass rate for o1-preview \+ DeepSeek \[Vendor or practitioner blog\]38), no published study has conducted an isolated SWE-bench ablation directly comparing *advice-only overseer injection* against *full subtask handoff* for identical trapped agent states.
